# IEEE Publication Baseline for Figures

This document summarizes public IEEE author guidance that can serve as a starting point. It is not a substitute for the current instructions of the target journal or conference.

## 1. What the official guidance says

The IEEE Author Center recommends vector artwork where possible because vector graphics remain clear when resized. It lists PS, EPS, and PDF as acceptable vector formats for journal graphics.

For non-vector graphics, the IEEE Author Center gives the following resolution guidance:

- Color and grayscale images: greater than 300 dpi.
- Black-and-white line art: greater than 600 dpi.

The Author Center lists common figure widths:

- One column: 3.5 in (88.9 mm).
- Two columns: 7.16 in (182 mm).

It also advises authors to create graphics at the required quality from the beginning; increasing the DPI of a low-quality raster image later does not restore lost detail.

Source: https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/create-your-ieee-journal-article/create-graphics-for-your-article/resolution-and-size/

## 2. File formats and fonts

The IEEE Author Center lists PS, EPS, PDF, PNG, and TIFF as accepted graphics submission formats. Microsoft Office formats are accepted only when the graphic was originally drawn in the relevant Office application. JPEG is listed for high-resolution author photos, not as a general figure format. The guidance also asks authors supplying EPS, PS, or PDF to embed fonts or convert text to outlines, and recommends consistent typography across figures and tables.

Source: https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/create-your-ieee-journal-article/create-graphics-for-your-article/file-formatting/

## 3. General communication principles

Proceedings of the IEEE advises that figures and tables should:

- communicate information clearly, accurately, and accessibly;
- be used selectively, when they strengthen the argument;
- be understandable without requiring the reader to search the main text for every detail;
- avoid unnecessary complexity and decoration;
- use consistent typography, spacing, labels, and panel conventions;
- avoid relying on color alone;
- use high-contrast combinations and avoid difficult red-green distinctions;
- define symbols, abbreviations, and color codes in captions where needed;
- include appropriate permissions and citations for reused or adapted artwork.

Its guidance gives approximately 9–10 pt as a recommended font-size reference. Treat this as guidance for that publication, not a universal font-size rule for every IEEE journal or conference.

Source: https://proceedingsoftheieee.ieee.org/resources/guidelines-for-figures-and-tables/

## 4. What must be checked for each submission

Before exporting, check the target venue's current instructions for:

- accepted file types;
- minimum resolution by artwork type;
- maximum dimensions and file size;
- column width and page layout;
- font embedding and font-size requirements;
- color mode and color-printing policy;
- multi-part figure handling;
- caption and panel-label conventions;
- supplementary-material requirements;
- permission requirements for third-party images or adapted artwork.

Do not assume that every IEEE publication has identical rules. When the target venue's instructions conflict with this baseline, follow the target venue.

## 5. Practical export defaults

When the venue has not yet been specified:

- keep diagrams, plots, and line art in editable vector form;
- export a PDF or SVG proof for review;
- export raster images at a resolution suitable for the target artwork category;
- set the physical figure size before raster export;
- inspect the file at the actual expected print size;
- keep source data and scripts for reproducibility.

These are workflow recommendations, not additional official IEEE mandates.
