# Visualization Ethics and Scientific Integrity

A visually attractive figure is not acceptable if it changes the scientific evidence.

## 1. Quantitative data

- Never fabricate measurements, sample sizes, error bars, confidence intervals, p-values, or significance marks.
- Preserve raw values unless a transformation is explicitly requested and documented.
- State normalization and the baseline used.
- Do not hide outliers or missing observations without a justified and disclosed rule.
- Do not truncate axes in a way that exaggerates differences.
- Use appropriate statistical summaries for the data type.
- Do not imply causation from correlation.

## 2. Microscopy, pathology, and medical images

- Preserve the original image data and provenance.
- Do not remove, add, move, or selectively conceal features that could change interpretation.
- Permitted display adjustments (for example, uniform brightness/contrast changes) must be applied consistently and documented according to the relevant journal policy.
- Do not clone, inpaint, or synthesize tissue, cells, bands, lesions, or other evidence.
- Keep raw images, segmentation masks, and overlays distinguishable.
- Maintain scale bars, orientation, ROI, and spatial registration.
- When combining images, disclose if they come from different sections, samples, fields of view, or acquisition sessions.
- Follow journal rules for contrast adjustment, cropping, and image manipulation.

## 3. Scientific illustrations

- Do not imply an anatomical, molecular, physical, or causal relationship that the source does not support.
- Label conceptual diagrams as conceptual and not to scale when needed.
- Distinguish observed data from a proposed mechanism or hypothesis.
- Use consistent labels and color semantics across panels.

## 4. Maps and spatial figures

- Preserve map projection, scale, coordinate reference, orientation, and geographic extent.
- Do not stretch or warp a map to fit a panel.
- Distinguish measured, interpolated, predicted, and simulated spatial layers.
- Disclose image registration and spatial transformations.

## 5. Reuse and copyright

- Do not reproduce a paper's full figure without permission or a license that allows the intended reuse.
- A paper being publicly accessible does not automatically make its figures reusable.
- Check the license and any third-party credit lines separately.
- Prefer original diagrams that explain the method in the user's own visual language.
- Cite the source when adapting a concept, dataset, or method.

## 6. AI-generated content

- Do not use generated imagery as a substitute for actual scientific observations.
- Do not generate fake microscopy, histology, gel bands, experimental images, or measured plots.
- AI-generated decorative or explanatory illustrations may be appropriate only when clearly conceptual, scientifically accurate, and permitted by the target venue.
- Use programmatic/vector tools for exact labels, quantitative data, and editable diagrams.
